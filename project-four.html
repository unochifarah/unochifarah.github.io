01 — Context

Evaluating Corporate Performance

This project analyzes 14 years of financial data collected from the annual 10-K reports of major publicly traded companies. Covering multiple industries from 2009 to 2022, the goal was to compare long-term profitability and industry health.

The analysis focuses on unpacking corporate profitability trends, long-term industry changes, and exactly how the 2020 pandemic shocked different sectors. It shifts from core data preparation in Excel to advanced interactive storytelling in Tableau.

Business Questions

  • — Which company has the highest net profit margin?
  • — Which industry has the lowest average profit margin?
  • — How has revenue changed year-over-year across industries?
  • — How did COVID-19 impact company revenues in 2020 compared to 2019?
  • — Which company has the highest gross profit margin?
Dataset

14 Years of Financial Data

A multi-industry financial dataset spanning 2009–2022, containing detailed metrics like revenue, costs, and profit margins for major public companies.

02 — Approach

From Raw Data to Visual Discovery.

Instead of jumping straight into visualizations, I started in Excel to clean the data and calculate the core financial ratios. Once the foundation was solid, I moved to Tableau to build interactive, cross-industry comparisons.

Step 01

Data Preparation

Before doing any analysis, I cleaned the dataset. I filtered out incomplete 2023 records so they wouldn't skew the historical averages, and manually calculated the necessary financial ratios in Excel to ensure my baseline metrics were accurate.

Step 02

Exploratory Pivots

I used Excel Pivot Tables to get a quick feel for the data. This helped me spot initial trends—like which industries consistently had the highest margins and where the obvious outliers were—before building anything visual.

Step 03

Visual Mapping

Once the data was clean and validated, I imported it into Tableau Public. My goal was to design charts that made it easy to compare massive tech companies alongside smaller industry players over a 14-year period without the visuals getting cluttered.

Step 04

Dashboard Integration

I built a central, interactive dashboard. Instead of static charts, I added filters and tracking mechanisms so a user could easily drill down into specific timelines, compare two specific companies, or isolate a single industry's performance.

03 — Process
01 / The Profitability Leaders

Finding the most efficient business models

Microsoft emerged as the clear leader, consistently holding the highest net profit margins. But the biggest surprise was McDonald's—proving that even in the food service sector, which typically has massive overhead and headcount costs, a highly optimized business model can drive huge returns.

Net Margin Operational Efficiency Benchmarks
02 / Where Margins Struggle

Identifying the toughest industries to profit in

Looking at industry averages revealed clear vulnerabilities. The Finance sector recorded the lowest overall profit metrics, while Manufacturing frequently struggled to stay in the green, often dipping into negative net income territory during market downturns.

Sector Analysis Margin Pressure
03 / The 100% Gross Margin Anomaly

Why understanding the business model matters

During my analysis, a bank (BCS) flagged with a 100% gross profit margin. At first glance, it looked like a data error. But a quick check revealed the context: financial services don't report a traditional Cost of Goods Sold (COGS). It was a great reminder that you can't blindly trust a metric without understanding the industry behind it.

Gross Profit Data Context Model Validation
04 / The 2020 Pandemic Shock

Who grew when the rest of the market dropped?

Tracking the 2020 timeline showed an immediate, widespread drop in revenue for almost every sector. Amazon, however, stood completely apart. While physical retail and competing entities faced massive demand drops, Amazon converted pandemic constraints into explosive growth.

05 — Key Findings
Market Profitability

Tech dominates, but fast food surprises.

  • —Microsoft consistently captures the top net profit margins.
  • —The IT sector secures both the largest revenue and highest profit growth over time.
  • —The Food sector reaches an impressive 22.6% net margin despite high headcount costs.
  • —McDonald's exhibits an incredibly optimized cost structure compared to standard retail.
Macro & Anomalies

Pandemic shocks and structural differences.

  • —Amazon stood uniquely resilient, growing significantly while the broader market contracted in 2020.
  • —Banks report 100% gross profits simply because financial services don't use traditional Cost of Goods Sold (COGS).
  • —Finance and Manufacturing displayed the lowest baseline profit margins across the 14-year period.

07 — Conclusion

What This Means for Business

The IT industry was the undisputed winner in both profitability and revenue growth between 2009 and 2022. While sectors like Finance and Manufacturing struggled with tighter margins, companies like Microsoft proved that highly optimized operations consistently drive exceptional financial performance.

The 2020 pandemic also served as a massive stress test. Amazon's ability to grow while physical retailers contracted highlights how different business models react to macroeconomic shocks. Ultimately, this analysis shows that revenue volume means very little if the underlying cost structure isn't built to scale.

08 — Reflection
Lesson 01

Clean the data before you chart it

Filtering out incomplete 2023 records early on prevented skewed averages. Bad data leads to bad charts.

Lesson 02

Understand the business behind the metric

A 100% gross margin looks like a mistake until you realize banks don't report Cost of Goods Sold (COGS). Context matters.

Lesson 03

Crisis accelerates market gaps

Macroeconomic shocks like the 2020 pandemic don't hurt everyone equally. They widen the gap between adaptable digital models and rigid physical ones.

Lesson 04

Headcounts don't always limit returns

High staffing requirements usually mean lower efficiency, but optimized service models like McDonald's can still turn high volume into consistent 22.6% profit margins.
